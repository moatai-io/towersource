# PBR

Trigger sentences are fully self-contained — do NOT paraphrase, abbreviate, or substitute variables.

## Trigger Sentences

**Software**:
> Review this plan separately from three perspectives — the end user who will interact with it daily, the on-call engineer who will debug it at 3AM, and the attacker who will try to exploit it — and give distinct findings for each.

**Business**:
> Review this plan separately from three perspectives — the customer who will decide whether to buy, the investor who will decide whether to fund, and the competitor who will decide how to respond — and give distinct findings for each.

**Research**:
> Review this plan separately from three perspectives — the peer reviewer who will evaluate methodological rigor, the practitioner who will try to apply the findings, and the ethics board member who will assess participant welfare — and give distinct findings for each.

**Education**:
> Review this plan separately from three perspectives — the student who will experience the learning journey, the instructor who will implement the curriculum daily, and the assessment evaluator who will judge whether objectives were met — and give distinct findings for each.

**Policy**:
> Review this plan separately from three perspectives — the affected citizen who will live under this policy, the bureaucrat who will implement and enforce it daily, and the opposition critic who will argue against it publicly — and give distinct findings for each.

**Creative**:
> Review this plan separately from three perspectives — the audience member who will experience the finished work, the collaborator who will help create it, and the published critic who will evaluate it against the field — and give distinct findings for each.
